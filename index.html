SciAgent Bench: example tasks

Six tasks from the accepted pool that show the three task tiers and the four properties we look for. Task and verifier descriptions only; the task packages, data and sealed answers stay private.

Scale AI × Georgia Tech updated 2026-09-28 descriptions abbreviated from the maintainers' READMEs

What we are looking for

Every task ships as a sandboxed environment with data, domain tools and code execution; a compute and wall-clock budget; a programmatic verifier; and ground truth established analytically, by expert work, or by a frozen evaluation suite. We look for tasks that are

  1. practical and useful for an existing domain: a passing solution would speed up research that is happening now;
  2. agentic by design: open-source tools, long-horizon multi-step work, files modified and files delivered, iteration on intermediate results;
  3. not impossible and not compute-heavy, but stumping current models: a few domain experts could solve them with the context given, and current agents mostly cannot;
  4. verifiable as a ground-truth final answer or as concrete rubrics.

Tier 1 simulated systems with known answers (about 40 % of the pool): data come from a generator, the answer is exact, fresh instances can be made on demand and confounds can be decorrelated from the target. Tier 2 expert analysis of real data (about 40 %): the agent runs a full analysis the way a domain expert would and is compared with an expert pipeline, a reference catalogue or an independent measurement. Tier 3 open-ended research problems (about 20 %): improve on existing work within a fixed budget, scored on a hidden panel the agent never sees.

How the pass rates were measured. Each task was run with three frontier coding agents, Claude Fable 5.1 (Claude Code), GPT-6 Astra (Codex) and Gemini 3.7 Flash (Gemini CLI), three independent trials each, inside the task's own sandbox and budget, and graded by the task's verifier. Mean pass@1 is passes divided by scored trials, averaged over the three agents. Provider-side refusals are excluded and reported separately. The reference solution passes every task whose reference is a maintainer method; for the three experimental-design tasks the shipped positive control is the strongest frozen comparator, which scores 0 by construction, and feasibility is shown by the agent passes.

The six tasks

optical-mapping-activation-maps Tier 2

Clinical, health and population: cardiac electrophysiology. Image and field data. Version 0.3.

From a raw voltage-dye camera stream of beating cardiac tissue, recover the per-pixel activation-time and APD80 maps an expert produces, with a tissue mask an expert would accept.

Reference
Delshad, Cherry et al., J. Physiol. 2023, 10.1113/JP283683 (method); an unpublished Fenton-lab recording (Georgia Tech) processed by the lab's expert.
Inputs
One 250 MB raw recording: 128×128 pixels, 16-bit, 529.09 frames per second, 7,620 frames covering 18 beats, with a vendor header and per-frame layout. The stream is stored transposed, frame 0 is under-exposed and the sign convention of the fluorescence signal is undocumented. The frozen definitions of activation time (50 % upstroke crossing, linearly interpolated), APD80 and beat detection are given. Python scientific stack, no internet. Withheld: the expert's activation, APD80 and mask maps.
Task
Segment the tissue and submit three 128×128 maps (tissue mask, activation time in ms, APD80 in ms), each averaged over the usable beats, together with the reproducible pipeline and a methods summary with fixed sections: approach, what the method targets, validation performed, budget used, limitations.
Verification
A deterministic grader scores the submitted arrays, so nothing the agent wrote is executed. Primary metric: activation-map RMSE in ms against the expert's map inside the intersection of the two masks after removing each map's median offset; APD80 RMSE is a gated secondary. Validity: the mask must cover at least 85 % of the expert's tissue with IoU at least 0.55, which rules out both the whole frame and the easy centre. Pass: valid, methods file present, activation error under one camera frame (1.89 ms) and APD80 error under two frames (3.78 ms), plus expert-likeness gates on the mask outline and the smoothness of both maps whose values are deliberately not disclosed to the agent. Anchors: a constant map 19.3 ms; wrong upstroke definition 5.5 ms; wrong polarity 6.1 ms; skipping denoising 2.5 ms and fails a gate; the reference pipeline 0.57 ms in about 10 s of compute; the beat-to-beat noise floor 0.3 to 0.7 ms. Shipped validity probes: label permutation, wrong definition, wrong polarity, no transpose.
Calibration
Fable 0/3, Astra 0/3, Gemini 0/3; mean pass@1 0.00. Fable and Astra reach 0.8 to 1.2 ms activation error, inside the frame gate, but hand in ragged masks and rough maps that an expert would reject and the expert-likeness gates catch. The reference passes with margin.

Why it is here. A real lab pipeline on real, unpublished data with the expert's own output as the ground truth (practical, verifiable as a final answer); cheap to run yet unsolved by all nine trials (stumping); a multi-step pipeline with file deliverables and a written self-validation (agentic).

phosphopeptide-evidence-inference Tier 3

Life sciences: phosphoproteomics. Spectra. GPU task (one A10G). Version 0.1.0.

De novo sequence phosphopeptides and place their phosphosites from tandem mass spectra, recovering more correct identities than the published decoder while holding the false-discovery proportion at or below 5 %.

Reference
Marx et al., Nat. Biotechnol. 2013, 10.1038/nbt.2585: synthetic phosphopeptide libraries with a designed phosphosite per pool (PRIDE PXD000138); the published InstaNovo-P de novo sequencing checkpoint as the baseline model.
Inputs
A development set of 6,302 spectra with truth, 221,825 labelled training spectra that share no peptide backbone with either panel, the InstaNovo-P checkpoint and its decoding code, working implementations of every comparator with their development scores, and the evaluation code. Sealed: about 13,500 spectra in blocks; the primary stratum is the synthetic library pools, whose designed phosphosite is the truth. Eight hours on 8 vCPU, 64 GB and one A10G.
Task
Submit a run.py that writes one prediction per spectrum (sequence, modification positions, a confidence in [0, 1] and an accept or abstain decision), a method.json naming the methodological change and the ablation that removes it, and a methods summary. The ablation variant must also run.
Verification
The submitted program is replayed on the sealed panel on a GPU with no network. Identities are canonicalised (I/L collapsed, common modifications normalised, phosphosites kept). Utility is the fraction of distinct correct identities recovered by accepted predictions, per block, averaged over blocks and strata; abstaining on everything scores 0. Gates: pooled false-discovery proportion at most 5 % (the operating point the source paper uses), at most 10 % in any stratum, precursor-mass consistency at least 95 %. Pass: against every frozen comparator (the published decoder as shipped and as tuned, a decoder ensemble, a recalibrated rescoring reference and a non-phospho de novo model) the paired stratified cluster-bootstrap lower bound of the yield difference exceeds a preset margin, and every gate holds. The recalibrated reference is packaged as the positive control and scores 0 by construction, because a copy of a comparator cannot pass.
Calibration
Fable 1/3, Astra 0/3, Gemini 0/3; mean pass@1 0.11. The passing run built a transductive library prior with precursor-mass-constrained candidate enumeration and reached 7.2 times the reference yield at 3.4 % false discoveries in 118 minutes. Every other scored run also beat the frozen decoders on yield but failed error control (false-discovery proportions of 6.7 to 20 %), which is exactly the discipline the task asks for.

Why it is here. Real public spectra, a published model and the field's own error-rate operating point (practical); an eight-hour GPU pipeline with code, ablation and written methods as deliverables (agentic); a relative comparator test combined with an absolute error gate (verifiable); one pass in nine (stumping).

strain-resolved-assembly Tier 3

Life sciences: metagenomics. Sequence data. Edition 0.2.0.

From long-read mixtures of two or three closely related bacterial strains, assemble correctly phased strain haplotypes that keep long-range allele linkage, beating today's metagenome assemblers and the shipped phasing baselines.

Reference
Complete RefSeq chromosomes of nonpathogenic strains (for example E. coli K-12 MG1655 against BL21(DE3), 99.2 % identity); reads simulated from them with pbsim3 (ONT R10-like, about 99 % accuracy, 12 kb). Assemblers under test: myloasm, metaMDBG, metaFlye.
Inputs
Five development mixtures, one per regime, as reads plus mixture metadata (strain count, abundances and species undisclosed) with full truth (strain genomes, read origins), a re-simulator to make more, the installed tool chain (myloasm, metaMDBG, Flye, minimap2, samtools, seqkit, racon), the comparators' code and development scores, and the evaluation code. Sealed: 15 mixtures across five regimes (97 to 99.9 % identity, 1:1 to 6:1 abundance, shared repeats, a recombinant mosaic, real strain pairs) built from genomes absent from development, about 1.7 Gb of reads. Eight hours on 16 vCPU and 64 GB.
Task
Submit a run.py that, for every mixture, writes the phased haplotypes (FASTA), the assembly graph (GFA), read assignments and a per-contig phase confidence, plus a method.json and a methods summary. An ablation variant must also run.
Verification
Offline replay under an unprivileged user. Contigs are aligned to each truth strain; a segmentation over an event grid splits them at misjoins and phase switches; each truth interval is credited once. Utility rewards long, correctly phased stretches (sum of squared segment lengths normalised by the strain length), averaged over strains and mixtures. Gates on the panel: falsely joined bases, phase switches per Mb, consensus quality (QV at least 30), duplication, false contigs and unaligned fraction. Pass: the paired stratified bootstrap lower bound of the utility gain exceeds a preset margin against every frozen comparator (default and tuned myloasm configurations, metaMDBG, metaFlye), and is positive against the packaged phasing baselines and an assembler-tuning control, with all gates holding. Anchors: perfect haplotypes 1.0; a single collapsed consensus 0.5; the best frozen assembler configuration is the reference and scores 0 by construction.
Calibration
Astra 0/3 and Gemini 0/3 under edition 0.2.0; Fable's three trials were refused at the first turn by the provider's safety classifier and are excluded. Mean pass@1 0.00 over six scored trials. Under the first edition one Astra run beat the assemblers after 221 minutes (31 of 33 strains recovered at 90 %, one phase switch, QV 45.5); the phasing controls added in 0.2.0 now also have to be beaten.

Why it is here. The most tool-heavy task in the pool: the assemblers metagenomics labs use today, driven from an eight-hour pipeline that delivers four files per mixture (agentic by design, practical); a sealed panel with frozen comparators and hard quality gates (verifiable); unsolved at the current edition (stumping).

rare-event-committor-transfer Tier 1

Mathematics, statistics and scientific ML: rare-event inference. Interactive simulation. Version 0.1.0.

Choose three shooting experiments on a two-lane, 15-site reversible ladder and infer the committor, the probability of reaching state B before state A, at ten states under two force settings outside the measured range, beating three frozen experimental-design baselines.

Reference
Marchi, Kang and Parrinello, arXiv:2410.17029; WESTPA 2.0, Russo et al. 2022, 10.1021/acs.jctc.1c01154. Solving the committor system at known parameters is easy; the task tests inference under a hard limit on shooting observations.
Inputs
The forward model (within-lane and cross-lane conductances set by two Gaussian barriers, an antisymmetric term and a linear force tilt), the full source of the three comparator methods, a local checker and a generator that makes development cases with truth. For each hidden case the verifier streams JSON lines: initial observations, the allowed actions (a state and a force in {−0.7, 0, 0.7}) and a budget of three queries; every query returns the number of successes out of 640 binomial shooting trials. Truth and seeds are withheld; each run draws 64 fresh cases from a 128-bit seed. Two hours on 4 vCPU and 8 GB.
Task
Submit a main.py that runs the loop for each case: issue exactly three distinct queries, then answer with 20 committor probabilities (two target forces of ±1.15, two lanes, five sites each).
Verification
Loss per case is the mean squared error of the 20 probabilities against the exact committor, scaled by a per-task target scale. Three frozen same-information comparators run on the same cases with the same observations: a fixed prior-linearised design with a nonlinear fit, a sequential Fisher-information design with a fit, and an importance-weighted posterior design and prediction. For each comparator the per-case loss differences feed a one-sided exact binomial sign test; pass requires p < 0.05/3 against all three together with a positive mean gain against each. Validity gates on shape, finiteness and range; malformed output scores 0; a copy of any comparator cannot pass. Fresh instances are generated for every run.
Calibration
Fable 0/3, Astra 1/3, Gemini 0/3; mean pass@1 0.11. The passing run used an importance-weighted calibration method and took 46 minutes; the other eight runs beat the fixed and sequential designs but not the posterior one. The shipped positive control is the posterior comparator itself, so it scores 0 by construction.

Why it is here. The cleanest Tier 1 recipe in the pool: exact generator truth, instances regenerated on demand so nothing can be memorised, and a closed experiment-design loop the agent has to drive (agentic); cheap and quick, yet it held eight of nine runs (stumping); a paired statistical test against strong frozen baselines (verifiable).

constitutive-protocol-information Tier 1

Chemistry and materials: rheology of viscoelastic materials. Interactive simulation. Version 0.1.0.

For each hidden material, choose three rheology frequency protocols that maximise the information about a three-branch Maxwell relaxation spectrum, and beat three same-information design baselines on the resulting information loss.

Reference
Villarreal et al. 2023, 10.1016/j.cma.2023.116282; Bhattacharya, Cao and Stuart 2026, arXiv:2603.12365. Adaptive and Bayesian design of experiments for constitutive models is established practice; the task asks for a method that beats competent implementations of it.
Inputs
The physics (a complex modulus with a baseline and three Maxwell branches, six unknown parameters), the comparators' source, a local checker and development cases with truth. For each hidden case: two initial frequency observations of the real and imaginary modulus with Gaussian noise, the menu of allowed protocols and a three-query budget; each query returns one protocol's noisy observation. Sixty-four fresh cases per run; truth withheld. Two hours on 4 vCPU and 8 GB.
Task
Submit a main.py that issues exactly three distinct protocol queries per case and ends with a six-parameter estimate. The estimate must be positive and finite, but it is the information gathered, not the estimate, that is scored.
Verification
The verifier forms the Fisher information matrix at the true parameters over the initial and chosen measurements (with a small ridge) and scores the loss as minus its log-determinant. The same three frozen comparators as above run on the same cases; the same rule applies: one-sided exact sign test with p < 0.05/3 against each comparator plus a positive mean gain, validity gates on shape, finiteness and distinct protocols, and an isolated execution environment.
Calibration
Fable 1/3, Astra 1/3, Gemini 0/3; mean pass@1 0.22, the highest of the six design tasks. Every evaluated run beats the fixed design; the posterior comparator stops the rest.

Why it is here. A materials-science instance of the same controlled-generator design (Tier 1 coverage in a second domain), with two agent passes that prove the bar is reachable and seven failures that show it is not low.

transient-isotopomer-experiment-design Tier 1

Life sciences: metabolic isotope tracing. Interactive simulation. Version 0.1.0.

Choose three positional 13C tracer mixtures for a transient labelling experiment, then predict the unseen mass-fragment measurements and estimate two carbon-routing fractions, beating three frozen design baselines.

Reference
INCA 2.0, Rahim et al. 2022, 10.1016/j.ymben.2021.12.009; Jadebeck et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.25079. Tracer design is the expensive step of non-stationary metabolic flux analysis.
Inputs
A three-carbon, atom-conserving transit surrogate: eight isotopomers, a direct route and two cyclic permutations mixed by two routing fractions, fast and slow pools with their time constants; measured channels are the intact three-carbon fragment and two two-carbon fragments with Gaussian noise. The comparators' source, a local checker and development cases with truth are given. For each hidden case: initial observations, the allowed tracer mixtures and three queries. Two hours on 4 vCPU and 8 GB.
Task
Submit a main.py that issues three tracer queries per case and answers with 23 values: the two routing fractions and a seven-bin fragment distribution at each target time for the target tracer, every block summing to at most one.
Verification
Mean squared error against the noise-free truth, scaled by the task's target scale, compared case by case with the same three frozen comparators under the same sign-test rule (p < 0.05/3 against each, positive mean gain) and validity gates.
Calibration
Fable 0/3, Astra 1/3, Gemini 0/3; mean pass@1 0.11. The passing run used a conditional design method; Fable's runs were even against every comparator and Gemini twice resubmitted the public posterior policy, which cannot pass.

Why it is here. A life-science generator task whose question a metabolic engineer actually asks before a labelling experiment (practical), with exact truth and on-demand instances (verifiable, contamination-resistant) and a single pass in nine (stumping).
